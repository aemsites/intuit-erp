A blueprint for growing a construction business

If you run a construction business, you know no project gets off the ground without a blueprint — and it’s just as difficult to plan and execute a goal to grow and scale a construction business without one. Nick Schiffer, an accomplished builder and host of the influential podcast The Modern Craftsman (over 4 million downloads), shared the hard-earned lessons from growing his firm, NS Builders, from $3 million to $10 million in annual revenue. In a webinar brought to you by Intuit Enterprise Suite, he focused on six key things he learned over the last two years — processes, reports, and tools he wished he had implemented earlier to protect margin, increase productivity, and create predictability. The core lesson: growing and scaling isn’t about more work, it’s about more profitable work.

Why leaders of growing businesses experience burnout

Schiffer was candid about hitting a wall two years ago. His team was pushing to get work done, but they didn’t have a holistic understanding of where the business really stood — he was building NS Builders as he went, without ever pausing to reflect on whether what he was building was good for himself or the business.

Without systems, growth really leads to burnout. And for myself specifically, about two years ago, that’s exactly where I was. I was doing more revenue than I am today. But I was burnt out and, frankly, just wasn’t making the profit that we should have been. The number one reason was visibility into my business, and I completely lost that visibility.

Nick Schiffer, Founder, NS Builders

A framework for success

The lessons Schiffer learned during that stretch became the framework used today at NS Builders. It centers on mastering job costing and financial clarity — knowing your numbers so you can improve them with strategic cost management — and building systems that scale, since scaling isn’t about doing more, it’s about doing it better and turning that growth into more profit rather than just more revenue. Just as important is avoiding the growth traps that kill margins, from mismanaging labor to over-promising clients, since mistakes made while scaling only compound if they aren’t corrected quickly.
