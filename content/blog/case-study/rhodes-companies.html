Managing a multi-entity education portfolio

Rhodes Companies serves as the family office for a diverse portfolio of businesses, with a primary focus in the education space. Among nine distinct entities, their flagship operation, Rhodes Graduation Services, stands as the nation’s largest Jostens representative. Rhodes’ day-to-day reality is complex, spanning distinct lines of business ranging from real estate and promotional products to a boutique branding agency.

We have nine sets of books, and leadership loves the ability to pull them all up in five minutes. They can even filter by entity to get exactly the view they want.

Caleb McDaniels, CFO, Rhodes Companies

The limits of a disparate SMB tech stack

As Rhodes Companies expanded, spinning up and acquiring new businesses every year, their financial tools struggled to keep pace. Using QuickBooks Online, the organization had nine siloed sets of books and no ability to consolidate financials. CFO Caleb McDaniels described financial operations that were continuously held back by manual friction — consolidating financials required the team to “pull down 9 different sets of information, and then manually create all of our reporting,” a process that hindered the team’s ability to focus on growth.

The business also lacked sophisticated controls and insights. Nearly 100% of vendors were receiving paper checks, with the only control being the owner signing them at the end. Their service-based entity, Rhodes Branding, suffered from “lumpy” financials due to annual contracts averaging over $50,000 being booked all at once rather than recognizing revenue over time.

Unifying a 9-entity ecosystem under one roof

Initially driven by a desire to bring their accounts payable “into the 21st century,” Rhodes Companies evaluated several vendors they believed could handle their complexity, including Avidxchange for AP automation and NetSuite for a full ERP, but ultimately ruled them out because “integration was going to be substantially more effort.” Rhodes selected Intuit Enterprise Suite because it offered the “benefits the big players have,” such as robust multi-entity consolidation and intercompany journal entry capabilities, but without the friction of a traditional ERP implementation.

It’s nice to not have to create a Frankenstein [situation]… and do it through 5 different platforms. It’s nice to just get that functionality all in one place.

Caleb McDaniels, CFO, Rhodes Companies

Driving efficiency and record breaking years

The move to Intuit Enterprise Suite drove immediate, measurable impact for Rhodes Companies. By automating intercompany journal entries and consolidations, the team can now allocate shared costs across all nine entities in one step, slashing month-to-end close time by 50%, from 10 days down to 5. Reporting that used to be completely manual is now near-instant, with executives able to pull reports in as little as 5 minutes. The team also revamped their annual planning with Spreadsheet Sync, shaving up to a week off the budgeting process.

Rhodes Branding saw the most significant financial turnaround: by using advanced accounting features for proper revenue recognition, the team moved away from “lumpy” cash-basis reporting to true accrual accounting, providing real-time insights into project profitability. The organization also now utilizes Intuit Enterprise Suite’s dual-level approval workflows, enabling business leaders to scrutinize and challenge invoices before payment to vendors.

Using AI as a “mini-auditor”

Going forward, Rhodes Companies plans to leverage the AI capabilities within Intuit Enterprise Suite to further streamline operations, especially spotting anomalies. McDaniels says the Suite’s AI insights once caught a 50% variance in a prepaid asset account — flagging a vendor subscription costing tens of thousands of dollars that had not yet been set up for monthly accrual. “It’s like having a mini-auditor,” he says.
