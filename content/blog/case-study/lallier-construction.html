From construction SMB to a true mid-market player

Lallier Construction (LCI), a family-owned construction leader across Colorado, reached a critical point in its growth journey a few years ago. With four separate entities and five distinct internal divisions, the leadership team realized they had outgrown the small-business label. While their legacy systems created manual bottlenecks, implementing Intuit Enterprise Suite provided the ERP-level infrastructure required to scale. By reclaiming up to 16-18 hours of accounting labor per week and unifying fragmented financial data, LCI has set a foundation to triple its revenue over the next three years.

Time sinks into manual processes and intercompany data management

Before adopting Intuit Enterprise Suite, LCI operated in a state of “manual legwork,” bogged down by a disjointed tech stack of Foundation for construction management and separate instances of QuickBooks Online that didn’t communicate. The biggest drain on resources was intercompany transactions: during the busy season, the team managed hundreds of invoices between LCI and its metal manufacturing arm, Western Architectural Metals (WAM), and had to record every transaction twice. Blake Rohm, Director of Finance, noted that on a busy week he was spending 10 to 20 hours simply ensuring intercompany data was correct, with critical insights like departmental break-even points buried in Excel spreadsheets.

You’d have to make sure you match up the invoices you were paying in LCI to the invoices you were receiving in WAM — a ton of work to just keep making sure you did the transaction in both sides.

Darla Hennessey, Controller, Lallier Construction

Moving under a central hub with Intuit Enterprise Suite

LCI’s leadership recognized they needed a central hub of operations where everything worked in and out of a single source of truth. Intuit Enterprise Suite’s ability to manage multiple entities and divisions, through features like intercompany journal entries and dimensions, made the shift an easy choice. The team moved away from manual duplicate entries to a multi-entity ERP that manages intercompany journal entries on one page, paired with QuickBooks Time and Payroll featuring geofencing for field employees so data flows seamlessly from the job site to the back office. For President Michael Lallier, the integrated ecosystem was a decisive factor in avoiding the “broken promises” of past vendors that struggled to provide the connectivity his business requires.

Data deep dives and a 90% efficiency gain

The transition to Intuit Enterprise Suite completely reallocated how the finance team spends its time. During the busy season, matching transactions and month-end reconciliation has plummeted from a high of 20 hours to just 2-4 hours per week — an 80% to 90% efficiency gain that lets the finance department move from data entry to true data analysis. The team now uses dimensions to break down LCI’s five divisions and generate accurate P&Ls for each, giving department heads the visibility to track work in progress and over/under billing without manual-entry errors.

Powering prosperity and employee ownership

With the financial foundation stable, LCI is focused on a vision to eventually hand the company over to its employees. The near-future roadmap includes full API integration of CRM and roofing software into the Intuit Enterprise Suite financial core, more advanced work-in-progress reporting, and a threefold increase in annual revenue over the next three years.

Run our business from just one central location.

Andy Coria, COO, Lallier Construction
