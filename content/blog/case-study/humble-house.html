From a kitchen blender to a 10,000-square-foot facility

Humble House Foods comes by its name honestly. Picture a couple of high school kids in 2008, mixing up homemade sauces with a kitchen blender, then selling those sauces at the local farmers market. Marsha Morales and her husband, Chef Luis Morales, aren’t high school kids anymore, and their 10,000-square-foot facility, which produces hot sauces sold through two major retailers across the United States, is hardly as humble as the rented 10-by-10 room where their dreams first took shape. At the time, the Moraleses tracked sales with a spiral notebook of tallies. Fast-forward to today, and the couple’s leap from pen and paper to QuickBooks Online, and most recently to Intuit Enterprise Suite, has made it possible for the team to measure the needs and successes of three different business entities, expand their sales goals while mitigating stress, and plan for a noteworthy future.

We would have to bottle our sauces with a spoon, and it was just him and me. I know that’s not very fancy sounding, but that’s where we came from.

Marsha Morales, Co-founder, Humble House Foods

Splitting into three entities multiplied the reporting headache

As Humble House grew, spreadsheets stopped keeping up, and the business split into three entities — manufacturing, wholesale, and retail — to see where money was being made or lost. Marsha took on the arduous task of managing all three in QuickBooks, spending hours manually pulling and matching reports across entities. She rarely got around to it except once a year, around tax time.

Reporting was the bane of my existence. In order to really see the health of your company, you need reports. But it was very difficult to do that for all three companies.

Marsha Morales, Co-founder, Humble House Foods

A bird’s-eye view in seconds, not days

With Intuit Enterprise Suite, Marsha can get a consolidated report within seconds, and her husband Luis, who isn’t as QuickBooks-savvy, can see the consolidated P&L or balance sheet any time through spreadsheet sync without ever logging in. The task of analyzing and creating consolidated reports by hand used to take Marsha four to six hours on a good day; now she can pull it up in 30 seconds. Intercompany eliminations mean she no longer second-guesses whether an entry belongs to the right entity.

With Intuit Enterprise Suite, you can get a consolidated report within seconds. My husband, who’s an owner but isn’t really QuickBooks savvy, doesn’t have to log in to see the numbers.

Marsha Morales, Co-founder, Humble House Foods

Keeping the Intuit tools they loved, plus food-safety traceability

Food manufacturing requires strict traceability, tracking every lot from raw material through work-in-progress to finished product. Humble House relies on a manufacturing software called Mar-Kov for that compliance work, and Marsha worried that upgrading off QuickBooks might break the integration. She and Luis looked at traditional ERP software, but it was expensive and still lacked functionality Humble House already relied on. In the end, they upgraded to Intuit Enterprise Suite and kept the Mar-Kov integration intact — the platform now syncs nightly, matching dollar values from raw ingredients to work-in-progress to finished goods.

In food manufacturing, you live or die by your profitability, and having that information available in real-time thanks to the link between Mar-Kov and our Intuit Enterprise Suite account is the reason we are able to stay successful.

Marsha Morales, Co-founder, Humble House Foods

Marsha’s advice: automate the tedious, focus on the business

For Marsha, who recently took her first vacation in over a decade, being able to step away from the daily grind is itself a marker of success.

Intuit Enterprise Suite has allowed us to automate a lot of the tasks that used to take up our time—especially the tedious ones. It’s given us the chance to maybe plan a new direction for Humble House.

Marsha Morales, Co-founder, Humble House Foods
