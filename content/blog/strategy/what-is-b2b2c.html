What is business to business to consumer (B2B2C)?

By Kai Des Etages

What is B2B2C? Business to business to consumer (B2B2C) is a business model in which a company sells its products or services to another business, which then sells to the end consumer.

Traditional B2B and B2C models often create operational silos, making it difficult to manage supply chains, maintain product variety, and meet evolving consumer expectations. But, the business landscape is shifting—companies no longer have to choose between selling to businesses or directly to consumers.

Instead, a hybrid business model is gaining traction: business to business to consumer (B2B2C). This approach blends the strengths of both traditional models, creating new opportunities for unlocking business growth and customer engagement.

In this post, we break down how B2B2C works, compare it to other models, explore its benefits and challenges, and highlight strategies for implementing it effectively. By the end, you’ll have a clear roadmap for leveraging B2B2C to expand your reach and streamline operations.

Understanding how B2B2C works

B2B2C leverages intermediaries—such as e-commerce platforms, software providers, or marketplaces—to bridge the gap between manufacturers, businesses, and consumers.

This strategy enables companies to expand their market reach without the complexity of direct customer management while still benefiting from brand exposure and customer insights.

B2B2C integrates elements of traditional business models:

  • B2B (business to business): Manufacturing and consumer packaged goods (CPG) companies sell to other businesses, often wholesale, focusing on long-term relationships and contracts.
  • B2C (business to consumer): Retailers sell directly to end consumers, typically using targeted marketing and personalized experiences.
  • D2C (direct to consumer): Manufacturers and CPG brands bypass intermediaries, controlling sales, branding, and customer data directly.

Successful e-commerce examples include Amazon, Instacart, and app stores. These businesses list their products while platforms handle consumer-facing interactions, transactions, and logistics.

B2B2C vs. other business models

B2B2C is unique because it combines elements of both B2B and B2C, offering a hybrid approach that allows businesses to scale efficiently while maintaining customer engagement.

However, it’s essential to understand how it differs from other models:

ModelCharacteristicsExample
B2BFocuses on long-term business partnerships, often involving bulk ordersA paint manufacturer sells exclusively to a home design company
B2CTargets individual consumers with direct sales and marketingA home design company sells bathroom remodeling services
D2CAllows manufacturers and CPG brands to control the entire customer experience, bypassing intermediariesA paint manufacturer sells directly to customers online and in its stores

A critical distinction lies in customer ownership. In B2B and B2C models, a single entity controls the customer relationship. In B2B2C, businesses share customer relationships with intermediaries. While this can increase reach, it also requires careful coordination to ensure consistent branding, service quality, and customer experience.

Advantages of B2B2C

One of the biggest advantages of B2B2C is its ability to expand market reach. Leveraging the established customer bases and distribution networks of partner businesses allows companies to access a much larger audience than they could on their own.

This allows for rapid scaling without the need for extensive direct-to-consumer infrastructure.

Another benefit is the ability to share resources. B2B2C partnerships provide access to logistics, technology, and expertise that would otherwise require significant investment. By working with intermediaries, companies can optimize their operations and improve efficiency while reducing costs.

Lastly, B2B2C enhances the customer experience by allowing businesses to leverage personalized marketing, streamlined purchasing processes, and improved delivery options. This leads to higher customer satisfaction and long-term brand loyalty.

Examples of successful B2B2C models

Amazon has built one of the most well-known B2B2C ecosystems, allowing businesses to list products on its marketplace while handling logistics, B2B payments, and customer service. This model enables businesses to reach millions of customers without needing their own e-commerce infrastructure.

Instacart connects grocery retailers with online shoppers, providing an easy way for consumers to order from multiple stores. By handling logistics and customer interactions, Instacart allows traditional retailers to expand their digital presence without building their own delivery networks.

Kayak aggregates travel services, allowing consumers to compare and book flights, hotels, and car rentals. Travel providers gain access to a large audience, while Kayak benefits from commission-based revenue. This model ensures a seamless booking experience for consumers while supporting businesses in the travel industry.

How to put a B2B2C strategy in place

To implement a successful B2B2C strategy, businesses need to build strong partnerships, align goals, and streamline operations. The following steps outline a structured approach for implementing this strategy and optimizing its impact.

1. Identify ideal partner businesses

The first step in establishing a B2B2C strategy is finding the right partners. Research businesses that complement your products or services, ensuring they have a solid customer base. When evaluating potential partners, assessing their brand values, target audience, and business objectives is crucial to ensure compatibility with your own.

Key takeaway: Choose partners that align with your brand and have a strong consumer reach to maximize the impact of your B2B2C strategy.

2. Establish partnership agreements

Clear and transparent agreements are vital for successful collaboration. Define each party’s roles and responsibilities to avoid confusion. In addition, set mutual goals that all parties can work toward, such as increasing market share or achieving specific revenue targets, to ensure alignment and shared success.

Key takeaway: Formal agreements and shared objectives set the stage for a productive and mutually beneficial partnership.

3. Integrate systems and processes

For a B2B2C strategy to run smoothly, businesses must integrate their systems and processes. Connecting IT systems for seamless data exchange, inventory management, and order processing is essential. Standardizing procedures across operations ensures consistency in customer experience, which is critical to building trust and loyalty.

Key takeaway: Streamlined systems and standardized processes enhance operational efficiency and provide a seamless customer experience.

4. Develop joint marketing strategies

Effective marketing strategies are key to promoting the partnership and expanding its reach. Consider co-branding initiatives that highlight the mutual value proposition and align your brand messages. Additionally, cross-promotion through each partner’s marketing channels will help increase visibility and attract a broader audience.

Key takeaway: Joint marketing efforts amplify your reach, strengthen brand recognition, and engage a wider customer base.

5. Monitor performance and optimize

Once your strategy is in motion, it’s crucial to track performance. Set key performance indicators (KPIs) to measure success, such as sales growth, customer acquisition, and satisfaction. Regularly review the progress with your partners and make adjustments to address challenges and optimize the strategy.

Key takeaway: Monitoring and optimizing performance ensures the long-term success of your B2B2C strategy by keeping the partnership on track and adapting to evolving market needs.

B2B2C best practices

To maximize the effectiveness of a B2B2C strategy, follow best practices that enhance operations and customer engagement.

Leverage technology: Streamline transactions by using integrated software that ensures seamless data exchange and improves operational efficiency. This technology enables businesses to simplify processes and offer better customer experiences.

Implement omnichannel sales strategies: Engage consumers across multiple touchpoints. This approach ensures your business is present wherever customers interact, creating a consistent and accessible experience.

Map the customer journey: Optimize every interaction. Understanding how consumers engage with your brand helps you identify opportunities for improvement and drive more meaningful connections.

Personalize experiences by using data: Tailor your marketing efforts and product offerings. This customization creates stronger relationships with customers and increases satisfaction and loyalty.

Align co-brand messaging with your partners: Ensure consistency in marketing. A unified message across all platforms reinforces your brand identity and strengthens the partnership’s impact.

Boost productivity and enhance profitability

B2B2C offers businesses a way to scale efficiently while maintaining consumer engagement. By leveraging partnerships and technology, companies can optimize operations and drive revenue growth.

B2B2C FAQ

How does customer service work in a B2B2C model and who handles what?

Typically, the intermediary handles frontline support through email or live chat, while the B2B provider manages product-related inquiries with the intermediary.

What metrics should be tracked to measure B2B2C success?

Key metrics for B2B2C include:

  • Sales growth: Indicates overall market traction and the effectiveness of both B2B partnerships and direct-to-consumer strategies.
  • Customer acquisition: Measures how well your business and its partners attract end customers, reflecting the strength of your distribution channels.
  • Retention rates: Shows long-term customer value and the effectiveness of both your product and partner-driven engagement efforts.
  • Engagement levels: Highlights how actively end consumers interact with your brand or products, helping assess partner performance and customer satisfaction.

Can a company operate both direct B2C and B2B2C models simultaneously?

Yes, companies can sell directly to consumers (B2C) while partnering with intermediaries to reach additional markets (B2B2C).

If the paint manufacturer also sold directly to consumers through its own website, that would be a direct-to-consumer (D2C) model.

Who owns the customer data in a B2B2C relationship?

Ownership depends on agreements. Some models share data, while others allow intermediaries to control customer relationships.

How do revenue-sharing agreements typically work in B2B2C partnerships?

Revenue splits vary based on contract terms, often involving commission-based structures or fixed fees for access to distribution channels.
