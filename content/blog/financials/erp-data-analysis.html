ERP data analysis explained: Data-based decision-making

By Marshall Hargrave

Enterprise resource planning (ERP) systems have become an indispensable tool for midsize and enterprise-level businesses—and for all good reasons. They streamline operations, manage resources efficiently, and provide valuable insights into business performance. But to unlock the full potential of ERP systems, you must take charge of data analysis.

ERP data analysis, also known as business analytics in ERP, involves collecting, organizing, and interpreting data from ERP systems to make informed decisions and drive business growth. With the help of ERP analytics, your business can gain a competitive edge, identify opportunities, and optimize operations.

Explore the fundamentals of ERP data analysis, its benefits, and practical examples to make better business decisions.

ERP data analytics explained

Data is the lifeblood of any ERP system. Every transaction and customer interaction creates a data point, which, over time, adds up to an enormous pool of information.

ERP data acts like a central hub, collecting and organizing this information from every corner of your business. Whether real-time sales reports, employee productivity metrics, or financial forecasts, your cloud ERP system stores everything in one place.

The three main types of ERP data include:

  • Transactional data: This type of data records the day-to-day business transactions within the ERP system. Examples include sales orders, purchase orders, invoices, and inventory movements. Transactional data is typically time-stamped and provides a detailed account of operational activities, making it essential for real-time reporting and tracking performance.
  • Analytical data: Analytical data is used for analysis and decision-making purposes. It aggregates and summarizes transactional data to provide insights into business performance. This type of data is often utilized in reports, dashboards, and business intelligence tools to help organizations identify trends, forecast future performance, and make informed strategic decisions.
  • Master data: Master data defines a business's core entities and serves as a reference point within the ERP system. It includes essential information about customers, suppliers, products, and employees. Master data is critical for ensuring consistency and accuracy across the organization, as it is used in various transactions and analyses throughout the ERP system.

But the real power lies in how you use that data. With the right tools and techniques, you can mine your ERP data to uncover insights that can improve decision-making, boost efficiency, and help your business adapt to change. It’s not just about collecting data—it’s about making sure you can analyze and apply it effectively through ERP analytics. This transforms information into a strategic advantage and helps you run your business smoothly.

Key ERP data analysis components

Understanding the key components involved in data analysis is essential to unlocking the full potential of ERP data. These elements work together to ensure that data is accessible and actionable.

1. Data extraction, transformation, and loading (ETL)

ETL is the foundational process of moving data from various ERP systems into a centralized data warehouse or data mart for analysis. It involves:

  • Extraction: Extracting data from ERP systems using various methods, such as APIs, database connectors, or file transfers
  • Transformation: Cleaning, standardizing, and transforming the extracted data to ensure data quality and consistency
  • Loading: Loading the transformed data into a data warehouse or data mart for analysis

2. Data warehousing and data marts

A data warehouse is a centralized repository that stores integrated data from multiple sources, providing a comprehensive view of an organization's operations. Data mart is a subset of a data warehouse, focused on a specific department or business function, providing tailored data for analysis.

3. Business intelligence (BI) tools

BI tools—like Power BI—offer interactive dashboards, reports, and visualizations to help you explore and analyze data. They enable your businesses to:

  • Monitor performance by tracking key metrics and identifying trends.
  • Analyze trends by uncovering patterns and correlations in data.
  • Make informed decisions by supporting decision-making with data-driven insights.

4. Advanced analytics (machine learning, AI)

Advanced analytics techniques, such as machine learning and artificial intelligence, can uncover hidden patterns and predict future outcomes. They enable businesses to:

  • Forecast future trends and events.
  • Recommend optimal actions based on data analysis.
  • Identify anomalies and potential fraudulent activities.
  • Group customers based on similar characteristics.

Benefits of ERP analytics for businesses

ERP analytics involves extracting meaningful insights from the vast amount of data generated by ERP systems. You collect, filter, and analyze data to identify trends, patterns, and anomalies that can inform decision-making.

ERP analytics offers a range of benefits that can transform the way your business operates. One of the key advantages of ERP analytics is its role in enterprise performance management, enabling businesses to track, measure, and optimize their performance with actionable insights from real-time data. Here are some of the other key benefits of ERP:

  • Improved decision-making: ERP analytics provides real-time insights, enabling data-driven decisions rather than relying on intuition. It allows businesses to make informed choices and respond quickly to market changes.
  • Enhanced operational efficiency: ERP analytics boosts productivity and smooths day-to-day operations by identifying inefficiencies and streamlining processes.
  • Optimized supply chain management: With real-time tracking and analysis of inventory and logistics, you can improve stock levels and reduce delays, ensuring a more balanced supply chain.
  • Enhanced customer satisfaction: Analyzing customer data allows businesses to tailor offerings and improve service, leading to higher satisfaction and loyalty.
  • Increased profitability: Better decision-making and operational efficiency reduce costs and waste, directly contributing to higher profitability.

Understanding the true power of ERP capabilities, such as invoicing and account management, and their benefits helps you make better business decisions.

ERP analytics use cases

ERP analytics is incredibly versatile, offering valuable insights across various aspects of your business. By applying ERP data to different functions, you can uncover opportunities, improve processes, and drive better outcomes. Let’s explore some of the key use cases where ERP analytics can make a significant impact.

  1. Financial analysis and reporting: ERP analytics help generate accurate financial reports, forecast revenue, and manage budgets effectively. They also provide a comprehensive view of financial performance, aiding in better strategic planning.
  2. Sales and marketing insights: ERP analytics analyze sales data and marketing campaigns to uncover trends and customer preferences. It helps in more effectively targeting efforts and optimizing sales strategies.
  3. Inventory management and optimization: ERP analytics processes track inventory levels in real time, helping you avoid stockouts and overstock situations. They also improve inventory turnover and reduce carrying costs.
  4. Supply chain visibility and efficiency: Real-time supply chain analysis helps identify bottlenecks and streamline logistics, enhancing overall supply chain efficiency and reducing delays.
  5. Human resources analytics: ERP analytics provides insights into employee performance, turnover rates, and workforce needs. It enables better HR decisions and more effective talent management.

How to perform ERP data analysis

Effective ERP data analysis requires a strategic approach. From selecting the right ERP system to ensuring data quality and fostering user adoption, each step plays a critical role in maximizing the benefits of ERP analytics.

Choosing the right ERP system

Look for a system that aligns with your business needs and offers robust ERP analytics capabilities. Consider scalability, ease of integration with existing tools, and the ability to customize reports and dashboards to suit your specific requirements.

Data quality and governance

High-quality data is the foundation of accurate ERP analysis. Establish clear data governance policies to ensure data is accurate, complete, and up-to-date. Also, implement processes for regular data cleaning and validation to prevent errors and maintain data integrity—essential for reliable analysis and reporting.

User adoption and training

Successful ERP implementation relies on user adoption and proper training. Ensure employees understand how to effectively use the necessary accounting software, like an ERP system, and know the benefits of data-driven decision-making. Don’t forget to provide ongoing training and support to help users leverage the system's full potential and integrate it into their daily workflows.

Continuous improvement and optimization

ERP data analysis is not a one-time setup but an ongoing process. Regularly review and refine your data analysis practices to adapt to changing business needs and emerging technologies. Don’t overlook the importance of regularly evaluating system performance and seeking user feedback. Make adjustments based on your analysis to improve the effectiveness of your ERP analytics and overall data strategy.

Best practices for ERP data analysis

Successful ERP data analysis requires adherence to these best practices like:

  • Overcoming data silos and integration challenges: Break down data silos by ensuring seamless integration across systems to provide a unified view of your data for comprehensive analysis.
  • Ensuring data privacy and security: Implement strong security measures, including encryption and access controls, to protect ERP data and regularly update protocols to address new threats.
  • Measuring ROI and justifying investment: Track key performance indicators (KPIs) to calculate the ROI of your ERP analytics. You can use this data to justify investments and guide future decisions.
  • Staying updated with emerging trends: Keep abreast of the latest trends and technologies in ERP analytics to continuously improve your practices and leverage innovations for a competitive edge.

ERP data analysis can significantly boost employee productivity and enhance profitability by streamlining operations and providing valuable insights into every aspect of your business.

Frequently asked questions

What is the difference between ERP and data analytics?

ERP is a comprehensive software system that integrates various business processes, while data analytics is the process of extracting meaningful insights from data. ERP systems collect and store data, while data analytics tools analyze and interpret that data to support decision-making.

What is the difference between ERP and SQL?

ERP is a business management software, while SQL is a programming language that helps interact with databases. ERP systems often use SQL to manage and query their underlying databases.

Is ERP just a database?

No, ERP is not just a database. While ERP systems rely on databases to store and manage data, they also include modules for various business functions, such as finance, human resources, and supply chain management.

Do you need both CRM and ERP?

ERP and CRM systems can often work together but serve different purposes. ERP focuses on core business processes, while CRM specifically manages customer relationships. Depending on your business's size and complexity, you may need both systems to effectively manage your operations and customer interactions.
