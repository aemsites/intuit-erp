What does a high-performing finance function look like at scale?

A high-performing multi-entity finance function is one in which financial data flows from the source to the consolidated report without manual intervention. According to Gartner, 75% of finance organizations are undergoing a major transformation to reach that point, but only 30% report successful outcomes.

Their aim is to move from being a legacy operation to an automated operation. Legacy firms still run manual operations, with finance spending most of the month producing the group number. At the 'transformed' stage, finance spends far less time putting the number together and more time investigating what it says, with anomalies reaching the CFO while there is still time to act.

Digital transformation in financial services has given $10M+ firms a clearer maturity path, from spreadsheet-based consolidation, through partial automation with disconnected tools, to touchless consolidation on a single platform. For multi-entity firms, the challenge is less about adopting new technology and more about getting the reporting infrastructure right.

1. Consolidated visibility and real-time data integrity

The first hallmark of a transformed finance function is the removal of information lag. In most multi-entity firms, this happens because each entity closes on its own timeline, using its own dimensions and rules.

This means finance has to manually assemble group numbers after the fact. Many companies struggle with this, as 54% of respondents to a Gartner survey said they faced issues producing trustworthy reports for stakeholders.

"Transformed" firms replace entity-by-entity spreadsheet merging with a single ledger fed by live transaction data across the group. This architecture eliminates manual data handling and reconciliations while ensuring that approvals are structured and enforced through automated workflows.

By moving from email-based oversight to system-driven governance, you maintain a rigorous audit trail without the administrative burden of manual follow-up.

With an automated, unified ledger, every adjustment carries a timestamp, an author, and a traceable path from entity-level entry to consolidated figure. External auditors spend less time reconstructing that trail, and the audit completes faster.

The same architecture lets you click a consolidated figure to see the source transaction at the regional level. You can trace any number back to its origin without requesting a separate report.

When you present the group result at close, every figure traces back to a verified source, and any question the board asks you can answer from the same ledger in the room.

Not all "real-time" consolidation is the same. Some systems batch-update overnight while others sync continuously. The distinction matters in multi-dimensional accounting because if you transfer cash from one entity to another, approaching a covenant breach, and the balances are eight hours old, the surplus you are drawing on may already be committed.

Eliminate the reporting gap

When financial data is available hours rather than weeks after period-end, issues such as an entity overspending against budget or a receivables position deteriorating across the group become visible while there is still time to respond. Real-time visibility allows you to reallocate working capital between subsidiaries to meet immediate operational needs. You correct problems in this quarter rather than explaining why they happened in the next quarter.

‘Transformed' firms use enterprise accounting software like Intuit Enterprise Suite to eliminate manual assembly from the process entirely. You get numbers that are ready for you to challenge, question, and form a view on before presenting them to the board.

This turns your next board meeting into a forward-looking conversation on capital allocation, entity performance, and growth priorities, using group numbers that everyone in the room trusts.

2. Centralized governance with distributed operational autonomy

The second hallmark of a transformed finance function is a governance framework that enforces global standards while allowing subsidiary managers to run their own operations.

Without that framework, each entity develops its own reporting conventions, coding structures, and approval habits. The PWC Global Compliance Survey found that 63% of executives said the disaggregated nature of data across their organization made compliance more difficult, rising to 70% in North America.

This fragmentation also means that the decisions you and the board take are only as accurate as the figures presented by the least disciplined entity in the group.

There is a difference between centralized control and centralized governance with local autonomy. The role of the financial controller in a multi-entity firm is to design and maintain a governance layer that scales without becoming a bottleneck.

Standardize global financial controls

A centralized platform enables the finance team to implement guardrails that automatically apply to the entire organization. Standardized categorization across all entities ensures that performance metrics are comparable across the portfolio. Without it, you can not rank entity performance or allocate capital based on like-for-like data.

You can also set up automated approval thresholds to ensure that significant expenditures require central sign-off, preventing regional budget variances from accumulating unnoticed. By establishing a single source of vendor data across the group, this prevents duplicate payments and strengthens your position when negotiating terms.

In Intuit Enterprise Suite, Dimension Assignment enforces the categorization structure across entities, and Parallel Approval in Workflow Automation routes expenditures above set thresholds for central review.

Every dollar of budget variance prevented through automated thresholds is a dollar that went exactly where you planned it.

Case study: Cornerstone Development, a construction firm managing five entities across eight-figure projects, engineering, and environmental compliance, was running on fragmented systems that left leadership without a reliable view of the business.

After adopting Intuit Enterprise Suite, the firm standardized all five entities on a shared chart of accounts with automated intercompany transactions. The CEO described the result as going from "flying blind" to "a clear windshield."

Role-based access

Granular permissions ensure that leadership maintains rigorous control over who can view, approve, or act on financial data across the entire portfolio. Rather than managing access at the local level—which often leads to oversight gaps—Intuit Enterprise Suite allows you to define roles based on specific financial responsibilities.

This architecture ensures that department leads have access only to the ledger data relevant to their performance, preventing unauthorized access to sensitive group-wide information.

Automated logging provides you with a direct view of user activity, ensuring transparency at every level. That keeps an access gap within a single entity from becoming a finding that delays your external audit.

Case study: Four Points RV Resorts operates multiple properties with up to three LLCs per site, and had no centralized way to manage who could access or approve what across entities. After migrating to Intuit Enterprise Suite, centralized roles and permissions enabled leadership to control user access across every LLC from a single location.

The team now manages permissions, approval routing, and user activity visibility across the group without maintaining separate access configurations for each entity.

3. Automated intercompany logic and predictive capacity

The final hallmark of a transformed finance function is the ability to turn complex multi-entity data into a strategic asset. In most growing groups, that potential is consumed by the manual work of managing intercompany activity.

When intercompany matching, adjusting, and elimination work is performed by hand, the finance function remains tethered to historical reporting. This manual "data assembly tax" consumes the capacity required for high-level strategy, keeping the team focused on backward-looking reconciliations rather than forward-looking growth.

Financial transformation reverses this dynamic by automating the mechanics of consolidation. When the system handles the transactional heavy lifting, the finance team can finally pivot toward predictive modeling and scenario analysis.

According to a Forrester TEI study commissioned by Intuit, a composite organization using Intuit Enterprise Suite projected 50% to 95% reductions in intercompany data-entry time and 60% to 95% reductions in reconciliation time over three years.

Every hour recovered is time your team spends testing whether the current quarter's forecast still holds.

Accurate neutralization of intercompany activity

Automated eliminations ensure that transactions between subsidiaries do not distort the firm's actual economic performance. When one entity sells to another, that revenue is real to the selling entity but not to the group.

If it stays in the consolidated number, the top line overstates what the business actually earned. Intercompany loans and transfers carry the same risk. Without automated reconciliation, balances are not offset at consolidation, leading the balance sheet to overstate what the organization actually owns.

In Intuit Enterprise Suite, Intercompany Expense Allocations and Dynamic Allocations handle the offset and distribution in the background, so the consolidated P&L and balance sheet reflect third-party economics. Every elimination is recorded and verifiable, ensuring that intercompany activity meets regulatory standards.

The group P&L you present stands up under lender diligence, covenant review, and board scrutiny, with every intercompany offset traceable and audit-ready.
