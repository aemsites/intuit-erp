5 essential features of a modern construction forecasting platform

With both in-office teams and on-site workforces handling everything from change orders, procurement, scheduling, and accounting, there are a lot of moving pieces to your construction company. The right construction forecasting software will bring them all together.

Here are the top five features to look for:

1. Real-time WIP (Work in Progress) automation

The numbers don’t lie: Two-thirds of construction projects fail to deliver on “all or most” time, cost, and quality objectives. According to Manon Bradley, Development Director at Major Projects Association, the key is effective performance management. And that means knowing where your progress stands at all times.

Your construction project forecasting software needs real-time WIP reporting to automatically calculate your percentage of completion (POC) by pulling data from all departments. With an exact account of labor hours and material installs, CFOs make mid-month course corrections before minor cost overruns become big problems.

2. Integrated change order management

Change orders average 10%-15% of the contract value, and can decrease labor productivity by as much as 20%.

With tools like Intuit Enterprise Suite, every single change order is captured, registered, and used to automatically update your project baseline, budget, and forecast simultaneously. Using a construction project forecasting platform ensures that no mid-project revision goes unnoticed, eliminating nonbillable extras that leave profit on the table once the job is done.

3. AI-powered labor productivity forecasting

Even with the rise of automation and other construction technology trends, a core element of the industry is still human. People aren’t machines, so their productivity can vary.

Worker experience, site management, and the availability of materials have the greatest influence on construction labor productivity, according to recent research—with technology adoption and weather conditions becoming increasingly important. The key is anticipating these productivity shifts.

AI-assisted construction forecasting can analyze current crew performance against historical benchmarks to predict labor overruns weeks in advance. Knowing how to adjust to seasonal changes and supply bottlenecks is the difference between calculating labor costs after the fact and making a precise prediction the moment the contract is signed.

Labor and labor burden costs can be as high as 40% of the total project cost. Knowing how to schedule your workforce efficiently and optimize for productivity can improve your bottom line.

4. Cash-gap and retainage modeling

US construction companies experience an average of 90 days sales outstanding (DSO)—nearly three months from when the project is completed to when it is paid in full. CFOs need a reliable projection of when revenue will come in and how much.

Effective construction budgeting and forecasting software includes detailed financial tracking and predictive estimates to ensure you have the liquidity to pay subcontractors and vendors before the owner’s check clears.

Intuit Enterprise Suite’s Cashflow Dashboard models Cash Out vs. Cash In, specifically accounting for the 10% retainage held by owners. With insights into payment history, cash conversion cycles, and transaction tracking, you can stay in the black and minimize cash crunches.

5. BI-powered scenario modeling

Whether it’s material costs, vendor inefficiencies, or labor productivity changes, business intelligence (BI) scenario modeling allows you to plan for both real-world trends and hypotheticals, rather than reacting to them.

With 94% of companies reporting BI as either critical or very important to their success, it’s a nonnegotiable feature of any construction forecasting software worth its salt.

Intuit Enterprise Suite allows you to test queries on emerging data and burgeoning trends, as well as mitigate your risk exposure to worst-case scenarios. With simple inputs like “What if material costs rise 5%?” or “What if we accelerate the schedule by 2 weeks?” you can form a proactive plan, pivot your strategy, and bid with confidence (instead of merely hoping for the best).

The field-first adoption

With the construction industry particularly prone to data silos, CFOs need forecasting software that all your employees will actually use daily—especially those on site. And that means having mobile-first interfaces.

On-site crews rarely have consistent access to desktops or laptops throughout the workday. Mobile-friendly platforms allow field teams to capture updates as work happens—feeding scheduling, cost tracking, and forecasting with current data instead of delayed reports.

Manual reporting relays waste time, creates room for human error and costly mistakes, and keeps project data limited only to a few individuals.

Instead, opt for a platform that puts your field team first, giving every team member transparent, direct visibility into progress and productivity.

This level of democratized data can incentivize teams to send more accurate data, more frequently. In fact, teams that use project and time tracking software complete 25% more tasks than those that don’t.

Audit your data flow

Using specific cost codes across projects allows CFOs to standardize comparisons and create a basis for accurate future cost estimation. If you allot a specific amount for electrical work, itemize each component with its own cost code—wiring, switches, labor, and so on.

Cost codes shouldn’t be limited to physical materials and your team. Create codes for specific subcontractors and specific duties based on historic charges. 2D drafting, for example, is a different skill set from 3D modeling and may require a different subcontractor with a different rate for each.

The more specific you can be with your codes, the more granular your data will become, and the easier it will be to see variances and overruns as they happen. Most importantly, use this data to communicate with your team, pivot your strategy, and reallocate funds.

In 2026, even a 48-hour delay in a simple change order can be the difference between a profitable job well done and a margin-busting cautionary tale.

For example, instead of using a single cost code for an umbrella term like “electrical parts” or “utilities,” a hypothetical manufacturing company could itemize these costs using sub-codes that correspond to the energy consumption of specific machines, seasonal or monthly HVAC costs, property taxes by subsidiary, and maintenance costs of specific lines. Similarly, CFOs can break down broad labor costs by department, role, and shift, with overtime and holiday hours in mind.
